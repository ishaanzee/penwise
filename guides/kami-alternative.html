Kami alternatives for filling and signing PDFs (free)

If your teacher assigns work through Kami, keep using Kami. But if you only need to fill in a worksheet or sign a form, or you installed Kami once to fill a PDF and it's still there, there are lighter free options.

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

First: Kami is the right tool for classrooms

Kami is built for schools. Teachers can assign PDFs, collect and grade student work, and connect to Google Classroom, Schoology, Canvas and Microsoft Teams. Kami has a free Basic plan with "10+ essential tools", and the classroom integrations come with its Teacher and School or District plans (Kami pricing, as of October 2026). It also has tools the alternatives below don't, such as voice and video comments and text recognition.

If your class runs on Kami, switching on your own will probably cause more trouble than it saves, because your teacher expects the work to come back through Kami. This guide is for two other groups:

  • Students who need to fill or sign a single worksheet or permission form and want something that doesn't lag or lose their work.
  • People outside school who installed Kami just to fill in a PDF, like a lease or a job application, and don't need any of its classroom features.

Why people look for a Kami alternative

Kami's Chrome extension has about 18 million users and a 3.82 out of 5 rating from 8,396 ratings on the Chrome Web Store (October 2026). The low-star reviews come back to a few themes:

  • Lost work. One reviewer wrote that it "doesnt auto save even though you select it, lost 5 hours of work".
  • Taking over PDFs. Another wrote that it "always sets itself as my default to view PDFs… Kami overrides it in a couple of weeks".
  • Pen feel. "Every stroke is 'wobbly'."

Those are individual reviews, not a measure of how often things go wrong. Millions of students use Kami every day without trouble.

If Kami lost your work, try this first

Before you switch, try to get the work back. These steps come from Kami's own help center:

  1. Restore deleted annotations. Open the file, click File → Restore annotations, choose what to bring back page by page (or Select All), then click Restore. Kami says this is free on any plan and works on annotations from any Kami tool (Kami: Restore annotations).
  2. If you were offline, Kami says your changes are "saved locally" and sync once you reconnect, and it recommends keeping the Kami tab open. If you closed it, reopen the file on the same device while you're online, then press save (Kami: Offline mode). Your work won't be on another Chromebook until it has synced.
  3. Make sure you opened the same copy. Kami saves to Google Drive, and exported files go into a "Kami Uploads" folder (Kami: Using Kami with Google Drive). If you opened the worksheet again from Classroom or an email, you may be looking at a different, blank copy.
  4. Email support quickly at support@kamiapp.com. The sooner they hear about it, the better.

To avoid it next time: press save before you close the tab or the Chromebook lid, and check the save indicator says it saved. This applies to every web-based PDF tool, not just Kami.

The alternatives

1. Chrome's built-in PDF viewer

Open the PDF in Chrome. The toolbar has Draw (pen and highlighter), Add text (with font, style and colour), an eraser, and undo. To sign, you draw your signature with the pen. Then use Download → With your changes, or Save to Drive (Chrome Help). Add text arrived in Chrome 151, which reached stable in July 2026 and is rolling out gradually (Chrome Enterprise release notes).

Good for: quick worksheets, especially with a freehand pen. Limits: no saved or typed signature yet (Google is working on one), no white-out, and no way to reorder pages.

2. The Gallery app on a Chromebook

On ChromeOS, PDFs can open in the Gallery app. It fills forms, adds text, highlights, has a pen and eraser, and can save a drawn signature to reuse. It can also rotate, reorder and delete pages, then save over the file or as a new one (Chromebook Help). Google notes the signature is "for convenience and doesn't provide signature verification or security", and that drawing over text "doesn't redact" it.

Good for: most students on a Chromebook. It's already installed, so it works even when you can't add extensions.

3. Penwise (Chrome extension and web app)

Disclosure: we make Penwise.

Penwise is for filling and signing PDFs. It's completely free: no paid tier, no account and no Google Drive connection. It runs fully offline in your browser, so the PDF is never uploaded, and it never takes over Chrome's PDF viewer. You open it when you want it.

  • Fill form fields, or add text anywhere on flat, scanned and non-fillable PDFs.
  • Check and cross marks, today's date, highlight and white-out.
  • Signatures drawn, typed or uploaded, saved on your device, plus initials.
  • Real redaction, organizing pages (reorder, rotate, delete) and merging PDFs.
  • Autosave: if the tab closes before you download, Penwise offers your unsaved work back next time. It stays on your device.
  • My details: save your name, address and so on, and add them in one click.

What Penwise doesn't have, compared with Kami:

  • No freehand pen or drawing tool. You can't sketch, circle answers or show working by hand. For math worksheets that need handwriting, Kami, Chrome's Draw tool or the Gallery app are better.
  • No teacher features: no assigning, grading, Google Classroom integration or comment threads.
  • No audio or video comments.
  • No OCR (text recognition).

Penwise also doesn't send your work anywhere. You download the finished PDF and hand it in yourself.

On a school-managed Chromebook

Schools often block installing extensions on managed Chromebooks, so you may not be able to add Penwise or any other extension. The Gallery app and Chrome's viewer are already there. The Penwise web app runs in a normal tab with nothing to install, and it should work if your school allows the site. Don't try to get around your school's settings. Ask your teacher which tool they want you to use.

Which should you use?

  • Your teacher assigns work in Kami: stay with Kami, and use the save tips above.
  • A handwritten worksheet on a Chromebook: the Gallery app or Chrome's Draw tool.
  • A form to type into and sign (a permission slip, application or lease): Penwise or the Gallery app. For more options, see how to fill out a PDF form for free and how to sign a PDF on Mac or Chromebook.
  • You're not in school and installed Kami just for one PDF: any of the free options will do. If you uninstall Kami, check that Chrome opens PDFs in its own viewer again.
