Chrome's built-in PDF editor: what it can do, and what it still can't (2026)

Chrome's PDF viewer has become a basic editor. You can fill forms, draw, highlight, add text and download the result without installing anything. For a lot of people that's all they need. Here's how to use it, and where it runs out.

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

What changed in 2026

For years, Chrome could only fill in PDF forms that already had fields. That changed this year:

  • February 2026: drawing, highlighting and drawn signatures were announced (BGR).
  • July 2026: Add text arrived in Chrome 151 (stable on 28 July), rolling out gradually on Windows, macOS, Linux and ChromeOS. Google pitches it for forms "that lack native fields" (Chrome Enterprise release notes).
  • In development: a "PDF Signing MVP" is being worked on in the Chromium issue tracker. Its planned pieces include a signature pad, a signature picker and saving a signature to your Chrome profile. There's no release date.

Because Add text rolls out gradually, you may not see it yet even on Chrome 151 or later. Check for updates under Settings → About Chrome.

How to use Chrome's PDF tools

Open the PDF in Chrome: drag it into a tab, or right-click the file and open it with Chrome. The steps below follow Google's Chrome help page (October 2026).

Fill in form fields

If the PDF has real form fields, click a field and type. Checkboxes and dropdowns work too. If clicking a blank line does nothing, the PDF is flat. Use Add text instead (below), or see how to fill a non-fillable PDF.

Add text anywhere

  1. Select Add text in the toolbar.
  2. Pick a font, style and colour in the sidebar.
  3. Click where the text should go and type.

Draw and highlight

  1. Select Draw, then choose Pen or Highlighter in the right panel.
  2. Pick a size and colour, then click and drag.
  3. Use Eraser to remove one of your marks, or Undo and Redo.

Sign

There's no Sign button yet. Select Draw → Pen, zoom in on the signature line so you have room, draw your signature with your mouse, trackpad or touchscreen, and select Done.

Save your changes

Select Download and choose With your changes. (Without your changes gives you the original file.) You can also choose Save to Drive, which puts the file in a "Saved from Chrome" folder. That doesn't work in Incognito or Guest mode.

Check the saved file. Open the downloaded PDF in a new tab before you send it, and make sure your text and signature are there. A bug report about Save As failing silently is open on the Chromium tracker.

Chrome also makes scanned PDFs searchable and selectable, using text recognition that runs on your device (Google).

The ones that matter most

Redaction. If you draw over an account number in Chrome, the number is still in the file underneath your mark. Anyone can copy it out. Google's own Chromebook help says drawing over text "doesn't redact". Preview has a real redaction tool, but it has had reports of it failing, so test the result whatever you use. Our guide on how to redact a PDF properly has a 30-second test.

Signatures. Drawing a neat signature with a trackpad is hard, and in Chrome you redraw it every time. Also know what a drawn signature is: a visual mark on the page, not a certified digital signature. ChromeStory makes the same point. That's fine for most leases, permission slips and HR forms, but if someone asks for a certificate-based digital signature, you need a different tool. More options are in how to sign a PDF without uploading it.

Pages. Chrome can't merge two PDFs, drop a blank page or put pages in a different order. On a Mac, Preview does this for free. On Windows and ChromeOS you need something else.

When Chrome is enough

  • The PDF has form fields and you just need to type into them.
  • You need a few words on a flat form and your Chrome has Add text.
  • You can sign with a drawn signature, and only occasionally.

If that's you, you're done. You don't need an extension.

When you need an extension

You'll want more than the built-in viewer if you sign often, need a typed or uploaded signature, need to hide information for real, or need to merge, reorder or delete pages. Be careful which extension you pick. Many PDF extensions upload your file to their website, and Chrome Web Store reviews are full of complaints about paywalls that appear at download time. One review of a merge extension says: "Only gives you one free merge before you have to pay!" (Chrome Web Store, October 2026). Before you install one, check whether it uploads files and what it charges for.
