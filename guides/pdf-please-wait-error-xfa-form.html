PDF says "Please wait..."? Here's how to open it

You open a government form and get a single page that says "Please wait... If this message is not eventually replaced by the proper contents of the document, your PDF viewer may not be able to display this type of document." Waiting won't help. The file is a special kind of form that most PDF viewers can't show, and the fix is to open it in a different program.

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

What the message actually means

The file is almost certainly an XFA form (XML Forms Architecture), usually built with Adobe's LiveCycle Designer. A normal PDF form has fields placed on fixed pages. A dynamic XFA form stores the form as XML inside the PDF, and the viewer has to build the pages from that XML when you open it. Pages can grow, sections can appear as you answer, and buttons can run checks.

To be safe, the author puts one ordinary placeholder page in the file, and that's the "Please wait..." page. A viewer that understands XFA hides it and draws the real form. A viewer that doesn't shows you the placeholder and stops. An Adobe forum answer from a former Adobe principal scientist sums it up: "XFA forms are not supported by anything other than the desktop versions of Adobe Acrobat Reader and Adobe Acrobat."

XFA is an old format. The PDF 2.0 standard moved it to its list of deprecated features (the Library of Congress format notes list it under features "dropped or deprecated in PDF 2.0"), but plenty of agencies still publish forms in it.

Fix 1: download it and open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader

This works for almost everyone with a Windows or Mac computer.

  1. Don't click the link to open it. That opens it in your browser's viewer again. Instead, right-click the link and choose Save link as (Chrome, Edge) or Download linked file (Safari). If it already opened in a tab, use the download button in the viewer's toolbar.
  2. Install Adobe Acrobat Reader if you don't have it. Adobe says it's "free to download and use without a subscription." You don't need the paid Acrobat Pro.
  3. Find the saved file, right-click it and choose Open with → Adobe Acrobat Reader. Double-clicking may still open it in Preview or your browser.
  4. Fill it in, save it, and reopen the saved copy in Reader to check your answers stuck.

This is exactly what Canada's immigration department tells people. IRCC's help page "I can't open my application form in PDF format" walks through saving the link and opening it with Adobe Reader, and says forms with a Validate button need "a computer with Adobe Reader 10 or higher."

Still seeing "Please wait" inside Reader? Check you're in the desktop app, not Reader's browser extension, and update Reader. If it still won’t open, download a fresh copy from the agency’s site rather than reusing one you saved from another viewer.

Fix 2: ask for (or look for) a different version

Many agencies offer another way in, so check the page you downloaded from before you install anything:

  • An online form or portal. Lots of applications that used to be XFA PDFs can now be done in an online account. This is often the easiest route on a phone.
  • A "print" or non-fillable version you can complete by hand or with a tool that adds text on top of the page.
  • The office itself. Ask whether they accept a different format. IRCC, for example, says visa application centre staff may ask you to fill the form in again at their kiosks.

Don't convert the form yourself unless the agency says that's fine. Some XFA forms, like IRCC's validated forms, add a barcode or check your answers, and a flattened copy loses that.

On a Mac

Mac Preview can't display these forms. Even if it opens something, it may look blank or empty: CIC News, writing about IRCC forms, warns that viewers "like Microsoft programs, or 'Preview' on Apple operating systems" may show them "as a blank file." Install Acrobat Reader for Mac and use Open with as in Fix 1.

On a Chromebook, iPhone, iPad or Android

Honestly, your options are limited. Adobe's mobile apps don't support XFA forms, and there's no desktop Acrobat Reader for ChromeOS. What you can do:

  • Use a Windows or Mac computer: a friend's, a library's, or one at work.
  • Use the agency's online form if there is one.
  • Ask the agency for a non-XFA version or another way to apply.
  • If the form is simple and doesn't need validating, a browser with XFA support (like Firefox on a computer) may display it. Check every page before you rely on it.

Some third-party mobile apps claim XFA support. We haven't verified them, so we're not recommending any.

Which government forms are XFA?

Fewer than you might think. Canada's IRCC immigration forms with a Validate button are the best-known case. They're built for Adobe Reader, and other viewers show the "Please wait" page or a blank form. Many US federal forms are not dynamic XFA. When we opened Form 1040 and Form W-9 from irs.gov and Forms I-130 and I-765 from uscis.gov in October 2026, all of them loaded as normal PDFs with ordinary fillable fields. If your form opens normally in Chrome or Preview, you don't have an XFA problem. See how to fill a PDF form for free instead.

Can Penwise open XFA forms?

Disclosure: we make Penwise.

No, and it isn't a fix for this error.
