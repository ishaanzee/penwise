Acrobat costs $14.99 to $19.99 a month. If you only fill and sign PDFs, you may not need it

Adobe Acrobat is the standard PDF tool, and it is priced like one. But a lot of people land on its pricing page because of a single form. Here is what Acrobat costs in 2026, what the free Reader already does, and what the paid plans actually add.

The early termination fee

The cheap-looking "$14.99/month" price is an annual commitment paid monthly. Adobe's pricing page says, for annual plans: "Fee applies up to half your remaining annual commitment if you cancel after 14 days." The month-to-month plan is listed as "Cancel anytime, no fee."

In practice: if you sign up for the annual plan, use it once, and cancel in month two, you can still owe a large part of the remaining months. People have complained about this on Adobe's own community forum, including one user who said the plan was worded as if it were month to month. Read the terms at checkout and set a calendar reminder if you take a trial.

Tip: month-to-month is not the same as cheap. It avoids the fee but costs $24.99 or $29.99 a month. For a one-off job, one month of that is still more than the one-time price of some alternatives below.

Free Acrobat Reader already fills and signs

This is the part pricing pages don't lead with. The free Acrobat Reader has a Fill & Sign tool. You can type into form fields, add text, and add a signature that you type, draw or upload. Then you save the file. No subscription.

Other free tools do the same job without any Adobe software: Mac Preview, Microsoft Edge's built-in viewer, and, since Chrome 149 (June 2026), Chrome's own basic fill, add-text and drawn-signature support. We walk through them in how to sign a PDF without uploading it.

Reader's downsides are real but small: it is a big install, and it nudges you toward paid features.

What paid Acrobat adds

Adobe's pricing page lists the paid features. These are the ones that matter, and the reasons to pay:

  • Editing existing text and layout. Changing a paragraph that is already in the PDF. This is the hardest PDF job, and free tools mostly can't do it.
  • OCR. Turning a scan into searchable, editable text. Adobe lists "turn scans into editable PDFs" under Pro.
  • Convert and export. PDF to and from Word, Excel and other formats.
  • Compare versions. Listed under Pro.
  • Collect e-signatures from other people and track the responses, with an audit trail.
  • Password protection, organizing pages and, on Pro, redaction and web forms.

If your job is on that list, Acrobat is a fair price for what it does. A free viewer will not replace it.

If you only fill, sign, redact or merge

Here is a plain way to decide:

  • Fill a form and sign it yourself: use Acrobat Reader, Preview or your browser. Free.
  • Fill a scanned or flat PDF: add text boxes on top of the page. Edge, Preview and Reader can all do this. See how to fill a PDF form for free.
  • Hide sensitive details: you need real redaction, not a black box. See how to redact a PDF properly.
  • Merge or reorder a few PDFs: a small tool is enough.
  • Edit text, OCR, convert, or get others to sign: that is Acrobat territory, or another full editor or e-signature service.

FAQ

How much does Adobe Acrobat cost per month in 2026?

As of October 2026, Standard is $14.99 a month and Pro is $19.99 a month on an annual plan billed monthly. Month-to-month is $24.99 and $29.99. Prices are before tax.

Is there a fee to cancel Acrobat early?

On annual plans, Adobe's pricing page says a fee of up to half your remaining annual commitment applies if you cancel after 14 days. Month-to-month is listed as no fee. Check the terms at checkout.

Do I need Acrobat just to fill and sign a PDF?

Usually not. Free Acrobat Reader has Fill & Sign, and Preview and browser viewers cover the basics. Paid Acrobat is for editing text, OCR, conversion, comparing versions and collecting signatures from others.
