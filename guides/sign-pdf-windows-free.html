How to sign a PDF on Windows for free

You don't need to pay for Adobe to sign a PDF on Windows 10 or 11. Windows already comes with a browser that can do it, and there are free tools for when you want a neater signature. Here are four ways, what each one can't do, and when you need something else entirely.

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

  • Quickest, nothing to install: open the PDF in Microsoft Edge and draw your signature.
  • A typed or image signature from a desktop app: Adobe Acrobat Reader is free and has Fill & Sign.
  • Already use Chrome: its PDF viewer can draw a signature too.
  • Sign often, want initials, dates and a saved signature, no install beyond a browser extension: Penwise (we make it).

Method 1: Microsoft Edge (built into Windows)

Edge comes with Windows, and its PDF reader can draw on a PDF. Microsoft says the inking tools let you "take notes, fill out and sign PDF forms" (Microsoft Edge team), and its Edge PDF documentation lists inking, ink colour and width, highlighting, text notes and basic form filling.

  1. Right-click the PDF in File Explorer and choose Open with → Microsoft Edge.
  2. If the form has fields, click them and type. For text on a page with no fields, select Add text in the PDF toolbar, click where it should go and type. Select the text box to move it or change the font (Microsoft).
  3. Select Draw in the toolbar. Use the arrow next to it to pick a colour and line width.
  4. Zoom in on the signature line and draw your signature with your mouse, touchpad, touchscreen or pen. Erase removes strokes if you need another go.
  5. Save from the PDF toolbar. Microsoft says you can "save a copy of the PDF with your highlights and markings" (Microsoft). Use Save as if you want to keep the original untouched.

Limits: it's a drawn signature only. There's no typed signature, and we couldn't find a way to place a signature image or save a signature to reuse, so you redraw it every time. A signature drawn with a touchpad can look shaky; a touchscreen with a pen works much better. Edge also doesn't support legacy XFA forms (Microsoft).

Check before you send. Open the saved file again and make sure your signature is on it. It takes five seconds and saves an awkward "it's blank" email.

Method 2: Adobe Acrobat Reader (free)

You don't need a paid Acrobat plan to sign. As of October 2026, Adobe's Reader page says Reader is "free to download and use without a subscription," is available for Windows 10 and 11, and that you can "draw, type, or upload an image of your signature" with the Fill & Sign tool.

  1. Install Acrobat Reader from adobe.com and open your PDF in it.
  2. Open the Fill & Sign tool. Where it lives depends on your Reader version, so look in the tools list or the right-hand panel.
  3. Choose to add a signature (or initials), then type, draw or import an image of your signature.
  4. Click to place it, resize it, and save the file.

Limits: it's a large install. Editing existing text and images isn't in Reader and needs a paid plan, according to Adobe. Adobe's online signing tool is a different thing: it uploads your file to Adobe and says "you must be signed in to complete the fillable form." For a private document, use the desktop app, and skip the "request signatures" option, which sends the file through Adobe's servers. Whether Reader is worth it compared with the other options is covered in what Acrobat costs and whether you need it.

Method 3: Chrome's built-in PDF viewer

If Chrome is your browser, open the PDF in it, select Draw → Pen, draw your signature, select Done, then Download → With your changes. Newer versions (Chrome 151 and later, rolling out gradually) also have Add text for flat forms. Like Edge, it's draw-only for now: no typed or uploaded signature and no saved signature, though Google is building a signing feature. Penwise is for when drawing your signature with a touchpad every time gets old. It runs in your browser, the PDF is never uploaded, there's no account, and it's entirely free with no watermark.

  • Signatures: draw one, type your name in a handwriting font, or upload a photo of your signature (the white background is removed). Save it on your computer and reuse it.
  • Initials and dates: add initials, today's date, and check or cross marks where a form asks for them.
  • Forms: fill form fields or add text anywhere, including on flat PDFs that don't have fields. Highlight, white-out and redact.

Install it from the Chrome Web Store, open the PDF in Chrome and click the Penwise icon. It doesn't take over Chrome's PDF viewer. The free web version works in any modern browser, Edge included, and opens the PDF inside the tab without uploading it.

What Penwise doesn't do: certificate-based digital signatures, sending a document to other people to sign, audit trails, OCR or file conversion. If you need those, use a different tool.

Visual signature or digital signature?

Everything above makes a visual signature: a picture of your signature placed on the page, the same as signing a printout and scanning it. That's what most leases, permission slips, HR forms and invoices need.

A certificate-based digital signature is different. It's tied to a digital ID, and the PDF records whether anything was changed after signing. Microsoft describes these as a way to show the content "has not been changed," and Edge can view and validate them, though it doesn't create them (Microsoft). Google makes the same point about its own tools: Chromebook help notes its PDF signing "doesn't provide signature verification or security" (Google).

You usually know you need one because the request says so: "digital ID", "certificate", or a signature box that won't accept a drawing. In that case, ask whoever sent the document which tool they expect. Often it's their own e-signature service, which sends you a link. If several people need to sign one document and you need a record of who signed when, that's an e-signature service too. Adobe notes that requesting signatures and audit trails "may require a paid Acrobat plan" (Adobe).

FAQ

Does Windows have a built-in way to sign a PDF?

Yes, through Microsoft Edge, which comes with Windows. Open the PDF in Edge, select Draw and sign with your mouse, touchpad, touchscreen or pen, then save. It's drawn signatures only.

How do I sign a PDF on Windows without Adobe?

Use Edge's Draw tool, Chrome's PDF viewer, or a tool like Penwise if you want a typed or uploaded signature you can reuse. None of them upload your file. If you'd rather not upload documents anywhere, see how to sign a PDF without uploading it.

Is Adobe Acrobat Reader free for signing?

Yes. Adobe says Reader is free without a subscription and its Fill & Sign tool can draw, type or upload a signature. Editing text and requesting signatures from others are paid.

Is a drawn signature a digital signature?

No. It's a visual mark. A certificate-based digital signature uses a digital ID and detects changes to the file. Ask the sender if you're unsure which one they need.
