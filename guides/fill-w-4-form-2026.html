How to fill out the 2026 W-4, step by step

A new job usually starts with a Form W-4. It tells your employer how much federal income tax to take out of each paycheck. Most people only need two of its five steps. This guide covers what goes on each line of the 2026 form, what changed in Step 4(b), and how to fill and sign the IRS PDF on your own computer so your Social Security number is never typed into a website.

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

This is a plain-language summary of the IRS form and its instructions, not tax advice. For the most accurate result, the IRS points you to its Tax Withholding Estimator. If your situation is complicated, ask a tax professional.

Get the 2026 form from the IRS

As of October 2026, the IRS About Form W-4 page links the 2026 W-4 PDF as the current revision. The form says "2026" in the top-right corner, and its footer reads "Form W-4 (2026) Created 12/8/25". Download it from irs.gov rather than a template site. Many employers collect the W-4 in their own payroll or HR portal instead. If yours does, use that, since the questions are the same.

The form says "Give Form W-4 to your employer." It does not go to the IRS. If you never hand one in, the form's privacy notice says you will be treated as a single person with no other entries.

The short version: most people fill Step 1 and Step 5

The form says to "Complete Steps 2–4 ONLY if they apply to you; otherwise, skip to Step 5." If you are single, have one job, have no dependents and don't want any adjustments, you fill in your details in Step 1, sign and date Step 5, and you're done. The other steps fine-tune the amount withheld.

Step 1: personal information

  • 1(a): first name and middle initial, last name, address, and city, state and ZIP.
  • 1(b): your Social Security number. The form notes that if your name doesn't match your Social Security card, you should contact the SSA so you get credit for your earnings.
  • 1(c): check one filing status: "Single or Married filing separately", "Married filing jointly or Qualifying surviving spouse", or "Head of household". The instructions say this sets the standard deduction and tax rates used for your withholding. Head of household is only if you're unmarried and pay more than half the cost of keeping up a home for yourself and a qualifying individual.

Step 2: more than one job, or a working spouse

Use this step if you hold more than one job at the same time, or you're married filing jointly and your spouse also works. Do only one of these:

  • (a) Use the IRS estimator. The form calls this the most accurate option, and says to use it if you or your spouse have self-employment income.
  • (b) Fill in the Multiple Jobs Worksheet on page 3, then enter the result in Step 4(c).
  • (c) If there are only two jobs in total, check the box on the W-4 for both jobs. The instructions say this works well when the two pay about the same, and withholds more than needed when they don't.

If you have several jobs, the form says to complete Steps 3 to 4(b) on only one W-4, and that withholding is most accurate if you do it on the W-4 for the highest-paying job.

Step 3: dependents and other credits

This applies if your total income will be $200,000 or less ($400,000 or less if married filing jointly). On the 2026 form:

  • 3(a): the number of qualifying children under 17, times $2,200.
  • 3(b): the number of other dependents, times $500.
  • Line 3: add them together, plus an estimate of any other credits you expect, such as education credits.

Page 2 says that to qualify for the child tax credit, the child generally has to live with you for more than half the year and have the required Social Security number. Filling in Step 3 means less tax withheld from each paycheck, so a bigger paycheck and a smaller refund.

Step 4: other adjustments (this is where 2026 changed)

  • 4(a) Other income: income that isn't from a job and has no withholding, such as interest, dividends or retirement income, if you want tax withheld on it. The instructions say not to include self-employment income here.
  • 4(b) Deductions: fill this only if you expect to claim more than the basic standard deduction. You get the number from the Deductions Worksheet on page 4. If you skip it, withholding is based on the standard deduction, which the worksheet lists as $16,100 single, $24,150 head of household and $32,200 married filing jointly.
  • 4(c) Extra withholding: any extra amount you want withheld each pay period, including the result of the Multiple Jobs Worksheet.

The 2026 Deductions Worksheet has new lines for deductions that people with tips, overtime or older parents may not expect. Each has an income limit. Here is what the form shows:

Privacy option. The form says that if you don't want to share other-job income for Step 2(c), you can use Step 2(b) instead. If you don't want to report other income in Step 4(a), you can enter a flat extra amount in Step 4(c).

Claiming exempt

You can claim exemption from withholding for 2026 only if you meet both conditions on page 2: you had no federal income tax liability in 2025, and you expect none in 2026. If you claim it, check the "Exempt from withholding" box, complete only Steps 1(a), 1(b) and 5, and leave the rest blank. The exemption is for one year: the form says you'll need to submit a new W-4 by February 16, 2027. It also warns that with no tax withheld, you may owe taxes and penalties when you file.

Step 5: sign and date

The form says it "is not valid unless you sign it." You sign under penalties of perjury that it is true, correct and complete. The "Employers Only" box at the bottom is for your employer, so leave it blank.

Fill it on your computer, without uploading

We downloaded the 2026 fw4.pdf in October 2026 and checked its structure. It is a fillable PDF with 48 form fields: 19 on page 1 (Steps 1 to 4, the checkboxes, the exempt box and the employer box), 6 on the page 3 worksheet and 23 on the page 4 worksheet. It has no field for the signature or the date, so you add those yourself.

Sending it to your employer

This part is general good practice, not an IRS rule. Your W-4 carries your Social Security number, so:

  • Use your employer's payroll or HR portal if there is one.
  • Avoid plain email. If you have to email it, password-protect the PDF and share the password another way.
  • Check who is asking. Fake "new hire paperwork" emails exist. Confirm the request comes from your actual HR contact.
  • Keep a copy somewhere safe, and delete stray copies from your Downloads folder.

Changed jobs, got married or had a child? The instructions say to complete a new W-4 whenever your situation changes in a way that would change the entries.

FAQ

Do I send Form W-4 to the IRS?

No. The form says "Give Form W-4 to your employer." Your employer uses it to work out how much federal income tax to withhold from your pay.

Which steps of the 2026 W-4 do I have to fill?

Everyone completes Step 1 (personal information) and Step 5 (signature). The form says to complete Steps 2 to 4 only if they apply to you. A single person with one job, no dependents and no other adjustments fills Step 1 and signs Step 5.

What happens if I don't give my employer a W-4?

The form's Privacy Act notice says that if you don't provide a properly completed form, you will be treated as a single person with no other entries on the form.

Do I have to sign the W-4?

Yes. The 2026 form says it is not valid unless you sign it. The IRS PDF has no field for the signature or date, so you add them yourself, by hand or as a signature image.

Can I claim exempt on the 2026 W-4?

Only if you meet both conditions on page 2: you had no federal income tax liability in 2025 and expect none in 2026. If you claim exemption, you complete only Steps 1(a), 1(b) and 5, and the form says you will need to submit a new W-4 by February 16, 2027. This is not tax advice.
